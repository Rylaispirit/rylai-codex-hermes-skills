# Academic Slide Design

> Portable Codex-Hermes replacement authored by Rylai.

1. Use one claim per slide.
2. Write titles as conclusions, not topic labels.
3. Make one exhibit visually dominant.
4. Keep body text readable from the back of the room.
5. Label axes, units, legends, uncertainty, and sources.
6. Use color to direct attention, not decorate.
7. Align content to a stable grid and preserve whitespace.
8. Explain equations and notation before asking the audience to interpret them.
9. Keep comparisons on the same scale.
10. Move dense tables, derivations, and secondary evidence to the appendix.

## Results Layou

Place the figure or table where it can be read at presentation size. Add a shor
annotation pointing to the evidence that supports the title. Do not rely on the
speaker to explain an unlabeled visual.

## Accessibility

- Avoid color-only distinctions.
- Use high contrast and colorblind-safe encodings.
- Add concise alt text or speaker notes when the delivery format supports it.
- Never shrink text to rescue an overloaded slide; remove content instead.
