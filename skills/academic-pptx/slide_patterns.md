# Academic Slide Patterns

These Rylai patterns are planning structures, not fixed templates. Select only
the patterns needed by the argument and adapt them to the supplied visual
system.

## Opening Frame

**Purpose:** establish subject, speaker, context, and tone.

**Include:** talk title, presenter, affiliation, venue, and date. A single
subject-relevant visual is optional.

**Check:** the title is readable at projection distance and does not compete
with decorative material.

## Problem in Contex

**Purpose:** show the concrete consequence of the problem.

**Structure:** one example, trend, failure case, or decision on the left; one
brief explanation of why it matters on the right.

**Check:** the importance is demonstrated rather than asserted with generic
language.

## Research Gap

**Purpose:** distinguish unresolved work from general background.

**Structure:** current capability, observed limitation, and the opening for this
study. A comparison table or annotated process often works.

**Check:** cited prior work is represented fairly and the gap does not rely on
an unsupported "first" claim.

## Research Question

**Purpose:** define what the work asks or tests.

**Structure:** one primary question with the relevant population, system,
comparison, outcome, or condition.

**Check:** the result section can answer the question directly.

## Contribution Map

**Purpose:** orient the audience to several linked contributions.

**Structure:** two to four contributions, each paired with its evidence or
artifact.

**Check:** contributions are concrete outputs or findings, not broad adjectives.

## Study or System Overview

**Purpose:** give the audience a map before details.

**Structure:** inputs, major stages, outputs, and evaluation. Use a directional
diagram with a single highlighted path.

**Check:** labels describe function, and arrows have an unambiguous meaning.

## Method Decision

**Purpose:** explain why one methodological choice affects validity or results.

**Structure:** choice, alternatives, justification, and consequence.

**Check:** implementation detail appears only when it changes interpretation.

## Experimental Design

**Purpose:** establish what was compared and how.

**Structure:** data or participants, conditions, baselines, metrics, and
evaluation protocol. Use aligned blocks or a compact table.

**Check:** sample size, split logic, exclusions, and repeated-trial details are
visible when relevant.

## Equation or Formal Model

**Purpose:** introduce a relationship that cannot be explained more clearly
with ordinary language.

**Structure:** one equation, definitions near the symbols, and one plain-language
interpretation.

**Check:** symbols are defined before reuse and notation matches the source.

## Primary Resul

**Purpose:** support one central finding.

**Structure:** message title, one dominant chart or table, one marked
comparison, and a short interpretation.

**Check:** units, uncertainty, sample size, and source are legible.

## Multi-Result Comparison

**Purpose:** compare outcomes across conditions without creating several
separate stories.

**Structure:** aligned small multiples or a matrix sharing scales and labels.

**Check:** order, scales, and color meanings stay consistent across panels.

## Robustness or Sensitivity

**Purpose:** show whether the conclusion survives a changed assumption,
parameter, dataset, or specification.

**Structure:** unchanged reference, one varied factor, outcome, and implication.

**Check:** the slide explains what would count as failure.

## Qualitative Evidence

**Purpose:** make behavior, errors, or mechanisms visible.

**Structure:** comparable examples at consistent size, annotated regions, and a
short statement of what the example demonstrates.

**Check:** examples are not cherry-picked without disclosure and privacy is
protected.

## Failure Analysis

**Purpose:** explain where the method or interpretation breaks.

**Structure:** failure category, representative evidence, likely cause, and
consequence.

**Check:** the failure materially informs limits or next steps.

## Literature Positioning

**Purpose:** show how the work relates to selected prior approaches.

**Structure:** a small comparison grid using criteria relevant to the research
question.

**Check:** absence of evidence is not displayed as evidence of absence.

## Limitation and Scope

**Purpose:** calibrate the claim.

**Structure:** demonstrated limitation, untested condition, and how each affects
interpretation.

**Check:** future work is separated from what the current study establishes.

## Synthesis

**Purpose:** combine several results into one answer.

**Structure:** research question, two or three evidence statements, conclusion,
and implication.

**Check:** no new evidence appears for the first time.

## Closing Frame

**Purpose:** leave a useful final image during discussion.

**Structure:** one-sentence answer, strongest supporting evidence, practical or
scientific implication, and discreet contact information when appropriate.

**Check:** the slide works without a generic "Thank you" dominating the screen.

## Section Transition

**Purpose:** reset attention between major phases of a long talk.

**Structure:** section name and one sentence explaining why the next phase
matters.

**Check:** transitions are rare enough to retain meaning.

## Appendix Answer

**Purpose:** support detailed questions without interrupting the main argument.

**Structure:** question as title, complete evidence, source, and concise answer.

**Check:** formatting and terminology match the main deck, and the slide can be
found quickly during questions.

## Pattern Selection Check

Before building, confirm:

- each pattern serves a step in the argument;
- repeated layouts are reused consistently;
- adjacent slides do not repeat the same evidence;
- visual form matches the type of reasoning;
- the main deck remains understandable without appendix slides.
